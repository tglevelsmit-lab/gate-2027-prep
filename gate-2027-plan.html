GATE 2027: a 25-week plan to 75+ marks

Starting 11 August 2026, you have roughly 25 usable weeks before the paper. That is enough for one careful pass over all ten subjects plus three revisions — but only if the first pass never stalls. This is the book list, the week-by-week route, and the mark arithmetic that gets you past 75.

01 The number you are actually chasing

In recent CS papers, ~66–70 raw marks lands near AIR 300 and ~75 lands closer to AIR 120–180. Targeting 76 gives you a cushion for one bad section on exam day. Here is where those 76 marks come from — this table is the plan's spine, and every hour below is allocated against it.

Subject Paper weight Your target Why this target
General Aptitude1514Cheapest marks in the paper. Nobody in the top 300 drops these.
Programming & Data Structures118Highest-weight core subject; C output questions are fully learnable.
Operating Systems97Formulaic and repetitive across years. Should be a strength.
Theory of Computation86.5Small syllabus, huge return. Closure-property questions are free.
Algorithms85.5DP and graph questions get genuinely hard; bank the standard ones.
Discrete Mathematics75.5Counting and graph theory are reliable; skip nothing here.
Computer Organisation75Cache and pipelining alone are worth ~4–5 most years.
Databases76Normalisation, SQL and B+ trees are near-guaranteed marks.
Computer Networks75.5Subnetting, TCP windows and CRC repeat almost yearly.
Linear Algebra · Calculus · Probability74.5Probability is the reliable half; calculus questions can be nasty.
Digital Logic65Number systems and floating point are pure practice marks.
Compiler Design53.5Parsing tables are mechanical; optimisation is where you lose.
Total~9776Roughly 78% accuracy across the paper.

Read this before anything else. Weights shift ±3 marks year to year, so no subject is safe to drop. What kills 70+ attempts is not a missing subject — it is a first pass that runs three weeks late and eats the revision phase. Protect the calendar over the completeness of any single topic.

The practice layer — also already downloaded

  • ~3,800 previous-year questions, 1987 → GATE 2026, split by subject and labelled by week. You will do three passes. Answers are included; explanations are not — each question carries a QR code to its discussion on gateoverflow.in, so plan on being online while solving.
  • 15 original papers, 2016–2026, plus 8 official answer keys, in exam format, held back for January.
  • Short notes you write yourself as you go. Not bought, not downloaded. Writing them is the revision.
  • The one thing still missing: a test series. It is the only paid item left, and nothing free replaces it — no question bank can tell you your percentile against the actual candidate pool, or how you handle a question nobody has seen before. Book one by early November.

03 Three phases

11 Aug → 29 Nov · 16 weeks

Build

One pass over all ten core subjects. Mathematics runs in parallel every single day rather than as its own block, and aptitude gets 30 minutes daily from day one. Every topic is closed with its previous-year questions the same day you learn it.

30 Nov → 3 Jan · 5 weeks

Consolidate

Revision round one, subject by subject, against your own notes. Complete previous-year sets solved end to end under time. Subject-wise tests from your test series, two per week, each followed by a written error analysis.

4 Jan → 5 Feb · 4.5 weeks

Peak

Twelve to fifteen full-length mocks at exactly the exam's time of day. Revision rounds two and three run off your notes and your error log only — no new material after 4 January, without exception.

04 Week by week

Two tracks run every day of the build phase: a main subject (about 5 hours: concepts plus that topic's previous-year questions) and a parallel maths thread (about 1.5 hours). Aptitude takes 30 minutes at the end of the day, always. Sunday is half study, half revision of the previous seven days.

05 The daily shape

Full-time · 8.5 h

  • 2.5 h — new concepts, main subject
  • 2.5 h — problems on that exact topic, previous-year questions first
  • 1.5 h — parallel maths thread
  • 1.0 h — spaced revision of the last 7 days' topics
  • 0.5 h — aptitude
  • 0.5 h — writing that day's entry in your own short notes

Working or in final year · 4.5 h weekdays, 9 h weekends

  • 2 h before work — new concepts
  • 2 h after work — problems on the same topic
  • 0.5 h — aptitude and notes
  • Weekends carry the parallel maths thread and the weekly revision in one block
  • This track reaches ~42 h/week against the full-time ~60. Expect the build phase to run about 3 weeks longer, so cut it back: drop generating functions, Quine–McCluskey and runtime environments to a skim, and start Phase 2 on schedule regardless

The one rule that decides the outcome. Never learn a topic without solving its previous-year questions the same day. A topic is not "done" when you understand it — it is done when you have solved every GATE question ever asked on it. This single habit is the difference between finishing the syllabus and scoring from it.

07 Exam day

65 questions, 100 marks, 180 minutes. Negative marking applies only to MCQs — one-third of a mark on 1-markers, two-thirds on 2-markers. NAT and MSQ questions carry no penalty.

  • Always attempt every NAT and MSQ. There is no cost. Leaving one blank is a pure donation.
  • Three passes. First 45 minutes: every question you can finish in under 90 seconds, aptitude included. Next 90 minutes: the 2-mark questions you know a route through. Final 45: the rest, plus a re-check of every NAT you entered — a misread decimal place is the most common avoidable loss.
  • Guess an MCQ only after eliminating two options. At 50/50 the expected value is positive; at a blind 25% it is not.
  • Never spend more than 4 minutes on a single question on the first two passes. Mark it and move. The paper is won on the questions you can finish, not the one you refuse to abandon.
